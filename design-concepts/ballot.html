How Columbus elects its City Council is on the ballot

This November, Columbus voters decide whether to keep the current hybrid system — where every voter helps elect all nine council members — or switch to true single-member districts, where you vote only for the member who represents your own district.

As of August 2026 · details may change before the final ballot is certified
What it is
A proposed city charter amendment
How it qualified
Citizen petition — 23,000+ signatures
When you vote
Tuesday, November 4, 2026
This is a developing story. City Council and the petition organizers have been discussing a possible compromise. What ultimately appears on the ballot — and its exact wording — may change. This page will be updated as the ballot is finalized. Every claim here links to a primary source.

The two systems, side by side

Both systems use nine geographic districts, and in both, a council member must live in the district they represent. The difference is who gets to vote for each seat.

Today · in place since 2023

Every voter in Columbus votes in all nine district races.

Proposed · the ballot measure
True single-member districts
Who votes for your member
Only residents of your district vote for your representative.
Races on your ballot
One council race — the seat for your district.
Who members answer to
Their own district's residents directly.
The argument for it
Neighborhoods get a representative accountable to them specifically.

How we got here

2018
Voters approve districts
Columbus ends its century-old fully at-large council, adopting the hybrid district system and expanding council from seven seats to nine.
2023
First district elections
All nine district seats are filled for the first time under the new system.
2025
Second cycle
Districts 1, 3, 4, and 7 are up; the staggered four-year terms take hold.
2026
A petition qualifies for the ballot
The group Our City Our Say gathers more than 23,000 signatures — clearing the validation threshold — to put single-member districts before voters. Council weighs a possible compromise.
November 4, 2026
Columbus votes
The measure goes before city voters.
If it passes
A new map and new rules
The city would implement the change on the timeline set by the amendment.

The compromise being discussed

Rather than a straight yes-or-no on single-member districts, City Council and some organizers have floated a middle path: expand the council with several new at-large seats alongside nine ward seats, pair it with campaign-finance reform, and redraw the district map. Supporters of a deal argue it delivers neighborhood representation without losing citywide accountability; some petition backers counter that it dilutes the original proposal. Whether a compromise reaches the ballot — and in what form — was still unsettled as of late August 2026.

What each side says

Supporters of single-member districts

  • Neighborhoods get a representative who answers to them directly, not the whole city.
  • Running for a single district costs less than a citywide campaign, lowering the barrier to entry.
  • It's how most large U.S. cities elect their councils.

Those who favor the current system

  • Every member being elected citywide keeps the whole council accountable to all of Columbus.
  • Voters keep a say in all nine seats rather than just one.
  • The hybrid system is only two cycles old — too soon to judge, some argue.

The points above summarize positions publicly stated by each side; they are not endorsements. Columbus Districts is a nonpartisan reference and does not take a position on the measure.
