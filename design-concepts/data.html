Methodology

How to read these figures

About the ACS 5-year estimates

The American Community Survey 5-year estimate is a rolling average over five years of surveys, not a single-year count. It's the most reliable Census source for areas the size of a council district, but because it's an average, it lags fast-moving change by a couple of years.

Why district figures are aggregated

Council districts don't line up with Census geographies, so every district figure here is built up from census block groups assigned to that district. Median income, rent, and home value are population-weighted averages of block-group medians — close approximations, not true district medians. Treat them as directional and comparative rather than exact.

ACS vs. Esri current-year estimates

Where available, each district page also shows Esri's current-year estimate for comparison. Esri projects forward from the 2020 Census using building permits and migration data, so in fast-growing districts it can run ahead of the backward-looking ACS average. Neither is "wrong" — they answer slightly different questions.

Known items under review. A small number of figures are flagged pending a boundary re-derivation from official council polygons — notably District 2's aggregated population and District 3's Asian-population count near the OSU campus, where block-group splits can distort a hand-built crosswalk. These will be corrected as the data pipeline moves onto the parcel-resolved boundaries.

Sources & corrections

Demographics: U.S. Census Bureau ACS via CivicWorth. Boundaries: official City of Columbus 2023 council map. Found an error? Every district page has a “Suggest an edit” form, and corrections are made quickly.
